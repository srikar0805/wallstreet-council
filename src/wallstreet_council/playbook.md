TRADER'S PLAYBOOK (shared knowledge; evidence beats vibes)

Base rates
- Over weeks, a single stock's move is mostly noise: daily volatility of 1-3% swamps any edge a council can find.
- Most active traders underperform simply holding the index after costs. The index is the bar every call must beat.
- Analyst consensus and price targets lag price and skew bullish. Treat changes (upgrades, downgrades) as more
  informative than levels.

Signals with some evidence (weak, not guarantees)
- Momentum: 3 to 12 month winners tend to keep outperforming modestly; it crashes hard in sharp reversals.
- Short-term reversal: extreme 1-week moves partly revert, especially on no news; less so on real news.
- Post-earnings drift: large earnings surprises tend to keep drifting in the surprise direction for weeks.
- Volume surges on a breakout above a long moving average confirm interest; surges on a gap down often mark
  forced selling.
- RSI above 80 or below 20 is stretched, not a timing signal on its own.
- Seasonality and "this month was usually up" are weak; never the main reason.

Risk rules
- Decide the exit before the entry: a stop level and what evidence would make you wrong.
- Size to the volatility: a 60%-vol stock needs a smaller position than a 20%-vol one for the same risk.
- Avoid holding through an earnings date unless the thesis IS the earnings.
- Beware crowded stories: when every headline agrees, the good news is usually priced in.
- Small caps, SME listings, penny stocks and stocks at circuit limits can be impossible to exit at a fair price.
- High yields and a strong currency are headwinds for long-duration growth stocks.

IPOs
- Hot, heavily oversubscribed issues often list up, but the first-day pop is not the long-run return; many IPOs
  lag the market over the following years.
- Look at pricing against listed peers, use of proceeds (fresh issue for growth versus offer for sale by exiting
  owners), promoter or insider selling, and anchor or lock-up expiries.

India specifics
- NSE and BSE trade whole shares, settle T+1, and many stocks have daily price bands (circuits).
- FII flows, the rupee, crude oil and the RBI's rate path move the whole market; watch them before single names.
- Results season, index rebalancing and derivative expiry weeks add volatility.

US specifics
- The Fed path, 10-year yield and dollar drive growth-stock valuations; earnings season drives single names.
- Pre-market and after-hours moves are thin and often fade.
